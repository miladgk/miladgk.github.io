Highlights

Optimal HiPIMS conditions are found at intermediate ion energy (70–100 eV).
Interfacial vacancy concentration exhibits unexpected non-monotonic composition dependence.
Atomic peening, ballistic mixing, and gas implantation compete during deposition.
Crystallization is delayed at high energies, then triggers rapid surface roughening.

Keywords
Nb thin film; Copper; Molecular dynamics; Magnetron sputtering deposition; Superconducting cavities of particle accelerators

Abstract
Improving the performance of niobium (Nb)-coated copper (Cu) Superconducting Radio Frequency (SRF) cavities is crucial for next-generation particle accelerators. Yet, performance is often limited by the reduction of the cavities’ superconducting properties at high accelerating fields, a behavior linked to Nb thin-film growth defects. Using large-scale molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, we systematically investigate the influence of high-power impulse magnetron sputtering (HiPIMS) deposition parameters, specifically ion energy and fraction of ionized species in the deposition flux, on the growth of Nb films on Cu(1 1 1) substrates. We find that high ion energies and ionization fractions induce significant surface roughening during growth, which is in agreement with the available experimental data. Moreover, our simulations reveal that this behavior is driven by the formation and rapid crystallization of a metastable amorphous Nb-Cu phase at the interface. Critically, we discover a nonmonotonic relationship between the composition of the amorphous layer and the concentration of residual vacancy defects. The defect density is minimized at an optimal composition of approximately 45% Cu, increasing for the interfaces with a lesser or greater amount of Cu compared to Nb. This reveals that the optimization of SRF cavities requires not only an increase in ion energy but also an accurate and careful tuning of deposition parameters to engineer the ideal interfacial composition, thereby minimizing the number density of performance-degrading defects.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we have employed comprehensive MD simulations to unravel the complex mechanisms governing the energetic deposition of Nb thin films on Cu substrates, to provide an atomistic roadmap for improving the SRF cavity performance. Our simulations demonstrate that high ion energy and ionization fraction lead to transient surface roughening. This is driven by the rapid crystallization of a metastable amorphous interface, whose composition is controlled by the deposition parameters.

Crucially, this atomistic finding provides a direct physical rationale for recent experimental observations in Nb-coated SRF cavities. Independent studies by Wang et al. [25] and Carlos et al. [26] demonstrated that cavity performance and critical current density reach a distinct optimum at an intermediate substrate bias of approximately $-75\text{ V}$, degrading at both higher and lower biases. Our simulation results are consistent with this empirical trend: the intermediate energy range of $70\text{--}100\text{ eV}$ (at $f_i \approx 33\%$) produces an interfacial composition near $\text{Cu}_{45}\text{Nb}_{55}$ that minimizes the retention of performance-degrading vacancy defects, while both lower and higher energies drive the composition away from this optimum and increase the defect density.

The main conclusion of this study is the identification of an optimal processing window for defect minimization. We revealed a nonmonotonic, U-shaped relationship between the Cu concentration in the amorphous interface and the final density of vacancy defects. The minimum number of defects occurs at an optimal composition of $\sim 45\%\text{ Cu}$. This insight fundamentally shifts the optimization paradigm from simply maximizing interfacial mixing to precisely controlling the deposition parameters to achieve this ideal composition. Conditions that require too low-energy deposition fail to create sufficient mixing, while those conditions consisting of too high-energy surpass the optimum point, creating a Cu-rich interface that is also prone to higher defect formation.

These localized vacancy clusters represent the atomistic nucleation stage of nanoscale void formation and can degrade both interfacial adhesion and SRF cavity performance. While our parametric variation of $f_i$ captures the fundamental atomistic effects of different flux compositions, explicitly modeling the macroscopic temporal structure of the HiPIMS pulse, including duty cycle and dynamic ion-flux evolution, represents an important direction for future multiscale simulation efforts.

Video 1. Time-resolved visualization of BCC formation during deposition at $f_i = 33\%$ and $E_i = 200\text{ eV}$. The animation shows an anisotropic amorphous-to-BCC transformation initiated at the critical thickness $\Theta \approx 8\text{ ML}$. Crystallization propagates along the growth direction while lateral spreading remains limited, producing columnar BCC domains and pronounced out-of-plane displacement.

Video 2. Time-resolved visualization of BCC formation during deposition at $f_i = 33\%$ and $E_i = 50\text{ eV}$. Early BCC nuclei appear at $\Theta \approx 2\text{--}3\text{ ML}$ and grow steadily throughout deposition, in contrast to the delayed, anisotropic crystallization observed at $E_i = 200\text{ eV}$.

Video 3. Time-resolved evolution of surface roughening during deposition at $f_i = 33\%$ and $E_i = 200\text{ eV}$. The surface remains smooth ($\sigma \approx 1\text{ \AA}$) up to $\Theta \approx 8\text{ ML}$ due to atomic peening which suppresses BCC nucleation and maintains a dense amorphous layer. Once the critical thickness is reached, a rapid amorphous-to-BCC transformation produces a sharp roughness increase ($\Theta \approx 9\text{--}14\text{ ML}$) and localized height variations up to $11\text{--}12\text{ \AA}$ by $\Theta \approx 22\text{ ML}$, consistent with stress-relief-driven hillock formation.

Video 4. Time-resolved evolution of surface roughening during deposition at $f_i = 33\%$ and $E_i = 50\text{ eV}$. Gradual surface-feature development and continuous roughness accumulation occur throughout growth. Early BCC nucleation ($\Theta \approx 1\text{ ML}$) and steady statistical roughening indicate morphology evolution governed by adatom mobility under moderate ion energies.
